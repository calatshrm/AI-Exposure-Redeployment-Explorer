How to read this tool

Estimates are occupation-level, derived from worker self-reports and smoothed across occupations with similar work activities. They describe exposure — how much of the work is already automated or AI-assisted, and whether nontechnical barriers stand in the way of displacement. They are not forecasts of job loss, and they say nothing about any individual employee.

Outcome definitions
Median Task AI Exposure
The median share of tasks that workers in the occupation report completing using AI tools.
Median Task Automation Exposure
The median share of tasks that workers report as automated — completed without human intervention.
Nontechnical Barrier Share
The share of employment with at least one nontechnical barrier to displacement: client preference, a legal or regulatory requirement, cost-effectiveness of human labor, or an organizational constraint. Nationally, 60.4%. Higher means more shielded.
High Automation Displacement Risk Share
The share of employment in the occupation where at least half of tasks are already automated and no nontechnical barrier to displacement is present. Nationally this is 5.1% of U.S. employment, about 7.9 million jobs.
Closest occupations
The 50 occupations whose work activities most resemble the selected one, ranked by similarity across the 41 generalized work activities in O*NET 30.3. Order reflects similarity of work, not wage, licensure, or geography.
Sources and limitations

Built on the demonstration dataset behind Automation, AI, and Job Displacement Risk in U.S. Employment (2026), SHRM, 2026: the 2026 SHRM Automation/AI Survey (n = 14,245, fielded April 2026), O*NET 30.3 generalized work activities, and BLS May 2025 Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics. Survey data are unweighted and self-reported; occupations with few direct respondents borrow information from similar occupations, so small-occupation estimates are the least certain. Deciles rank occupations against each other, not against an absolute standard. "Closest occupations" indicate similarity of work activities only — they are not validated career pathways and carry no assumption that a worker could move without training, credentials, or a pay change.

No employee-level or personally identifiable data is used or collected by this tool. Selections stay in the browser.
